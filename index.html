Remittances

When money is on its way and you want to find it

A remittance that has not shown up in the account is usually traceable. Which address to write to depends on how it was sent.

Sent to IOB by SWIFT, an exchange house or an overseas branch

Write to the foreign department with the details below. The more of them you include, the faster the trace.

fedcar@iobnet.co.in
  1. Date, currency and amount of the remittance
  2. Reference number, if sent through an exchange house tied up with IOB or an IOB overseas branch
  3. SWIFT message details, or a copy of the message, if sent by SWIFT
  4. Originating country and the sending bank with its SWIFT BIC
  5. Name of any intermediary bank, with its SWIFT BIC
  6. Name and address of the remitter
  7. Beneficiary name, the 15-digit account number, and the IOB branch holding the account
  8. Any special instruction — for example to open an FCNR(B) deposit, or not to convert the currency

Converted by another bank and credited by NEFT or RTGS

If the remitter's bank or another Indian bank converted the currency and sent rupees straight to the account, the trace belongs with the RTGS cell instead. Quote the UTR number.

rtgscell@iobnet.co.in

Before you write. Have the UTR from the sending bank, the date and amount, and the beneficiary account number ready. Without the UTR a NEFT or RTGS credit is difficult to place.

Inward remittance charges are nil across all three NRI savings tiers, so a shortfall in the credited amount is normally the work of an intermediary bank abroad rather than a charge at this end.

Coming home

What happens to the accounts when you return

Returning to India permanently changes your residential status, and the accounts have to follow. The path runs in both directions.

  1. 1 You return to settle in India

    After at least one year abroad, you can open a Resident Foreign Currency account without any approval from the Reserve Bank of India.

  2. 2 NRE and FCNR balances move into RFC

    What you held in those accounts at the time of return can be transferred across and stay in foreign currency, instead of being converted to rupees on a single day's rate.

  3. 3 Overseas income keeps flowing in

    Income from assets you still hold abroad, the proceeds when you sell them, and the whole of a pension received from abroad can be credited to the RFC account.

  4. 4 If you go abroad again, it reverses

    Funds in the RFC account can be transferred back to NRE or FCNR accounts. Interest on RFC is taxable while you are resident, so the move back matters.

Questions

What NRI customers ask

Is the interest on my NRE account really tax-free?

Interest on NRE accounts is exempt from Indian income tax, and the balance is fully repatriable. Interest on NRO and RFC accounts is taxable. That single difference is the main reason to keep overseas earnings in an NRE account and Indian income in an NRO account rather than mixing them.

How much can I send out of my NRO account?

Current income such as rent, dividends, pension or interest can be remitted abroad, and beyond that up to USD 1 million per financial year, which runs April to March, for bona fide purposes and subject to the bank being satisfied with the documentation.

I moved abroad. What happens to the account I already had?

It is redesignated as an NRO account. You do not close it and start again — the existing account continues under the non-resident rules.

Can I close an FCNR deposit early?

Yes. Interest is then paid at one percent below the rate applicable for the period the deposit actually ran. If the deposit is closed before it completes one year, no interest is paid at all.

What is NRI Plus, and which account does it sit in?

NRI Plus is a one-year rupee fixed deposit scheme for NRIs, priced above the bank's ordinary deposit rate: 9.06% annualised up to Rs. 10 lakh and 9.12% above it. Like any rupee deposit it is booked under your NRE or NRO account, so the tax and repatriation rules of that account still apply. Deposit rates are revised periodically, so confirm the rate on the day you book.

Can a resident relative be on the account with me?

Yes, in several cases. NRE accounts may be held jointly with a resident on a former-or-survivor basis, NRO accounts jointly with a close resident relative, and FCNR deposits jointly with a resident close relative on former-or-survivor. An NRE nominee may be a resident Indian.

Can someone in India operate my account for me?

An NRE account can be operated by a power of attorney or mandate holder for making local payments. It is the usual arrangement for NRIs who need bills and commitments in India handled while they are away.

Does any of this need Reserve Bank approval?

Opening an NRO account for an individual or entity of Bangladeshi or Pakistani nationality or ownership requires prior approval from the Reserve Bank of India. Opening an RFC account on return to India does not require approval.
